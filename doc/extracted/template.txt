[Heading 3] Inserting a Table of Contents
[No Spacing] In order to insert an automatic table of contents, you will have needed to apply styles to your headings (as shown above). Once your headings have been assigned a style, you can then insert a table of contents that will automatically populate with the correct heading level indentations, leader dots, and page numbers. This will also create an embedded link from your table of contents to the section in your project in both a MS Word doc and in a PDF file.
[No Spacing] To insert the table, follow these instructions:
[No Spacing] In Ribbon above, click References
[No Spacing] Click ‘Table of Contents’ on the left hand side of the ribbon
[No Spacing] Choose ‘Custom Table of Contents’ below the built-in tables
[No Spacing] After a Table of Contents box pops up, click “OK”, without making any changes within the box.
[No Spacing] Typically, inserting a table of contents, list of figures, and list of figures should be the last step in the formatting practice. If you’ve made changes to content that moves a heading to a different page number, all you’ll need to do is go back to your inserted table of contents, right click, select ‘Update Field’, and select “Update page numbers only”. This will make changes to your page numbers and won’t affect the headings.
[No Spacing] To create a table of contents you will need to assign a heading style to each heading in your entire document then follow the steps below:
[No Spacing] Locate your Table of Contents page above
[No Spacing] In the Ribbon above, click References
[No Spacing] In the Table of Contents section, click Table of Contents
[No Spacing] Look past the built-in Table of Contents and select Insert Table of Contents
[No Spacing] Under the General section in the Show Levels box, select the number of headings you want to show in the Table of contents – 3 levels are common, but it can incorporate all levels, if you wish.
